# AI Opportunity & PRD Generation: Turning Real-World Problems into Buildable AI Ideas

> **Don't start with "What can I build with AI?" Start with "What real problem can I solve better with AI?"**

## 1. Introduction

AI is everywhere, but that doesn't mean every problem needs AI.

Strong AI ideas usually start with a **real problem**, not a cool technology.

For example:

- A support team spends hours answering repetitive questions.
- Employees search through hundreds of documents for information.
- A business manually reviews thousands of records.
- Developers repeatedly perform the same analysis or documentation tasks.

These are potential **AI opportunities** because AI may reduce time, effort, or complexity.

```text
Real-World Problem
        ↓
AI Opportunity
        ↓
AI Solution
        ↓
PRD
        ↓
Buildable Product
```

---

## 2. The Core Idea

An **AI opportunity** is a real-world problem or workflow where AI can create meaningful value.

The important part is the word **problem**.

Don't begin with:

> "I want to build an AI chatbot."

Begin with:

> "What problem are people struggling with?"

Then ask:

> "Could AI solve or improve part of this problem?"

AI can create value by helping to:

- Save time
- Reduce repetitive work
- Improve decision-making
- Handle large amounts of information
- Personalize experiences
- Generate content
- Retrieve knowledge
- Predict or classify information

AI should be the **means to solve the problem**, not the reason for building the product.

---

## 3. Finding AI Opportunities

Some workflows naturally contain opportunities for AI.

| Problem Pattern | Possible AI Opportunity |
|---|---|
| Repetitive tasks | Automation |
| Large amounts of text | Summarization / extraction |
| Difficult information retrieval | RAG / AI search |
| Repeated customer questions | AI assistant |
| Manual classification | AI classification |
| Content creation | Generative AI |
| Complex decisions | AI-assisted decision support |
| Predictable patterns | ML prediction |

A useful question is:

> **"Where are people spending time doing something that a machine could assist with?"**

## 4. From Problem to AI Idea

Once we find a problem, don't immediately start coding.

First understand the problem:

```text
Real-World Problem
        ↓
Who has this problem?
        ↓
Why does it matter?
        ↓
How is it solved today?
        ↓
What could AI improve?
        ↓
AI Opportunity
        ↓
AI Solution
```

### Example

**Problem:** A company receives hundreds of customer-support questions every day.

**Current approach:** Support agents manually search documentation and write responses.

**Pain point:** A lot of time is spent answering repetitive questions.

**AI opportunity:** Use AI to retrieve relevant company information and draft responses.

**Potential solution:** An AI support assistant.

The progression is:

> **Problem → Pain Point → Opportunity → Solution**

## 5. What Is a PRD?

**PRD** stands for **Product Requirements Document**.

It describes what a product should do, who it is for, and what requirements it needs to satisfy.

For an AI product:

```text
Business Problem
       ↓
User Needs
       ↓
AI Capability
       ↓
Product Features
       ↓
Technical Requirements
```

A useful AI PRD should answer:

- Who is the user?
- What problem are we solving?
- What should the product do?
- Where does AI fit?
- What are the main features?
- What does the user flow look like?
- How will we know the product is successful?

A PRD doesn't need to be a massive document.

Its purpose is to give the team **enough clarity to build the right thing**.

---

## 6. Generating an AI PRD

A simple AI PRD can contain:

### Problem Statement
What problem are we solving?

### Target Users
Who experiences the problem?

### Proposed Solution
What are we building?

### AI Capability
Where exactly will AI be used?

### Core Features
What should the product be able to do?

### User Flow
How will users interact with it?

### Requirements
What does the system need to accomplish?

### Success Criteria
How will we measure whether it works?

Think of the PRD as the bridge between an idea and a buildable product:

```text
Idea
 ↓
Problem
 ↓
Users
 ↓
Solution
 ↓
Features
 ↓
Requirements
 ↓
Success Criteria
```

## 7. Real-World Application

Imagine a university where students repeatedly ask administrative questions:

- "How do I apply for a transcript?"
- "When is the admission deadline?"
- "Where can I find the scholarship requirements?"
- "What documents do I need?"

### Problem

Administrative staff spend significant time answering repetitive questions.

### AI Opportunity

Students need quick access to reliable university information.

### AI Solution

Build an AI assistant that retrieves information from official university documents and answers student questions.

### PRD Snapshot

**Product:** University AI Assistant

**Target Users:** Students

**Core Features:**

- Ask questions in natural language
- Retrieve relevant university information
- Provide concise answers
- Reference source documents
- Handle common administrative queries

**AI Capability:** RAG can retrieve relevant information from university policies, handbooks, FAQs, and other documents before generating an answer.

This is much stronger than simply saying:

> "Let's build a university chatbot."

We have identified the **problem, users, opportunity, AI capability, and product direction**.

## 8. Common Mistakes & Best Practices

### Common Mistakes

**❌ Starting with the technology**

"I want to build something using GPT."

Start with the problem instead.

**❌ Adding AI where it isn't needed**

If a simple search or automation solves the problem, AI may not be necessary.

**❌ Making the idea too broad**

"Build an AI system for education" isn't a buildable product.

Narrow the problem.

**❌ Ignoring the user**

A technically impressive product can still fail if it doesn't solve a meaningful user problem.

**❌ No success criteria**

If you can't measure improvement, it's difficult to know whether the AI solution actually works.

### Best Practices

- 🎯 Start with a real problem.
- 👤 Clearly define the target user.
- 🔍 Understand the existing workflow.
- 🤖 Use AI where it provides genuine value.
- 📦 Keep the first version focused.
- 📏 Define measurable success criteria.
- 📝 Use a PRD to turn the idea into something buildable.

Let's give an AI a real-world problem and ask it to turn that problem into a buildable AI idea.

```text
A small online store receives around 200 customer
```

```text
Target Users:
Customer-support teams and online shoppers.

AI Capabilities:
- Natural-language understanding
- RAG for company policies and FAQs

Core Features:

Success Criteria:

- Improve average response time
```

# Key Takeaways

> **Great AI products start with great problems, not great prompts.**

Remember:

1. **Start with the problem, not the technology.**
2. **Identify who experiences the problem.**
3. **Find where AI can create genuine value.**
4. **Turn the opportunity into a focused solution.**
5. **Use a PRD to make the solution buildable.**
6. **Define success before building.**

The goal isn't to build **something with AI**.

The goal is to build **something useful because of AI**.
